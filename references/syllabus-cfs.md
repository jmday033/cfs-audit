# CFS assignment: BUS 624, Fall 2026 (paraphrased)

Paraphrased from the CFS section of the Fall 2026 BUS 624 syllabus. Check the syllabus itself for exact wording and any updates. The CFS is 25% of the course grade.

## The ask

The company's Budget Committee asks your group for a memo. Teams choose one of two options:

1. **Uncertainty option.** Prepare for uncertainty in one area (e.g., inflation, elections, AI, policy changes): the "as-is" (what the company does now) and the "what-if" (what it can do to improve), with recommended actions and how much to invest.
2. **Commodity option.** Plan for changing commodity prices: the "as-is" (based on current prices) and the "what-if" (what changes if the price of oil moves **75% in either direction**), with what you'd change and how much it will cost.

Either way: do an in-depth analysis of the financial statements first and use it to support the what-if, and analyze the downstream negative impacts and how to mitigate them (the syllabus example: operating cash falls by about $Y for 5 years, investment Z is delayed 2 years, mitigated by action W).

**AI policy:** AI tools are allowed, but pull and verify the original source documents and treat AI output like an analyst report, not an end product. The instructor wants to see what you add beyond AI. The company must report under U.S. GAAP.

**Competitors:** two to three right-sized competitors, plus industry averages (UH library resources).

## What each step must contain

### Step 1: Overview, MC, MD&A, AR
- What the company does and interesting "to knows."
- MC and MD&A: two to three takeaways each that support your initial strategies. AR: two to three takeaways (there are two audit reports: internal controls and financial statements).
- Explain in your own words, as if answering a neighbor; don't read from the 10-K.

### Step 2: Common-size analysis
- Combined horizontal and vertical analysis of the balance sheet and income statement, most recent 3 years.
- Compare with the two biggest competitors and with industry averages.
- AI may assist, but verify by hand.
- Presentation: two to three takeaways covering trends, significant increases or decreases, differences vs. competitors and industry, and strengths and weaknesses.

### Step 3: Ratios
- Select and compute **15 ratios** that best support your strategies (textbook Exhibit 4-4, pp. 4-36 to 4-38, and pp. 6-26 to 6-27).
- Compare with industry averages and competitors.
- Presentation: two to three takeaways covering trends, comparisons, significant changes, strengths and weaknesses, and how the company is adapting.

### Step 4: Draft strategies and recommendations, incl. negative impacts
- Downstream impacts (financial, ethical, operational) and how to mitigate them.
- Qualitative and quantitative support; SMART strategies; an implementation timeline.
- Decide the amount to invest; estimate initial investment, training and maintenance costs.
- Identify and compare **at least two** strategic options: operational changes, costs, timeline.
- Explain the logic for the impacts you chose and for each mitigation (e.g., reskilling, phased investment, contingency planning).

### Final memo
Executive Summary; Purpose; Background; Discussion/Analysis; Recommendations (advantages, disadvantages, how to minimize the disadvantages); Conclusion. Each person adds an individual "what I learned" note. Email the project, the company website link and the 10-K links to the instructor.

## Chapter prompts (completeness check for Steps 2–3)

Summarized from the syllabus's additional instructions:

- Ch. 4: 3-year common-size statements with industry averages; major expense categories; pre-tax and after-tax margins; ROE broken into ROA, margin and leverage; capital intensity; leverage.
- Ch. 4–5: profitability ratios for 3 years; major revenue sources; revenue recognition; unusual items; effective tax rate; deferred taxes.
- Ch. 5–6: liquidity and turnover ratios; receivables; inventory method; fixed asset turnover; depreciation methods.
- Ch. 7: solvency ratios; debt interest rates; swaps; 4-year debt maturities; credit rating.
- Ch. 8: classes of stock; issuances; treasury stock; dividends; ROE, payout ratio and dividend yield for 3 years; book vs. market value; stock compensation.
- Ch. 9–10: consolidation; equity-method investments; intangibles; leases and their maturities; pensions.
- Cash flow statement: major non-cash items; main investing and financing activities.
- Auditor: who, any change in 3 years, the opinions.
- Recent news and webcasts: anything new since year-end. Would you invest? Would you work there?

# Ratio definitions as taught in BUS 624

Paraphrased summary of the Module 4 lecture ("Analyzing Financials", Fall 2026) and the textbook it follows. Use these definitions before any general or AI-default definition: a ratio computed the course's way should not be marked down for differing from another source. When a team uses a different definition, it isn't wrong if the slide says so. Flag it only if the definition isn't disclosed or is mixed across peers.

Tag: `[course]` = from the course slides; `[textbook]` = from the textbook pages the syllabus cites (Exhibit 4-4, pp. 4-36 to 4-38; pp. 6-26 to 6-27). Check the slides and textbook for exact wording.

## Conventions `[course]`

- **Averages.** Balance-sheet denominators use the average of the opening and closing balances, e.g. average total assets or average equity. A team using year-end balances should say why (e.g., no prior-year balance after an acquisition).
- **Noncontrolling interest.** ROE uses net income to common shareholders over average equity. With preferred stock, take out preferred dividends and preferred equity.
- **The NCI ratio.** The full ROE breakdown has a third factor for noncontrolling interest. For most S&P 500 companies it's close to 1, so ROE = ROA × financial leverage is accepted.

## Return on equity and DuPont `[course]`

| Ratio | Definition |
|---|---|
| ROE | Net income ÷ average stockholders' equity |
| ROA | Net income ÷ average total assets |
| Financial leverage (FL) | Average total assets ÷ average stockholders' equity |
| ROE breakdown | ROA × FL (× NCI ratio) |
| Profit margin (PM) | Net income ÷ sales |
| Asset turnover (AT) | Sales ÷ average total assets |
| ROA breakdown | PM × AT |
| Adjusted ROA | ROA with interest expense removed, because operating managers control operations but not the amount of debt |

Points the course stresses:
- Higher leverage raises ROE but adds risk; the trade-off should be stated.
- **Buybacks shrink equity and inflate ROE.** For companies buying back stock, the course points to ROA or a breakdown of leverage as the cleaner read. A draft that praises a high ROE driven by buybacks deserves a margin question.

## Margins and productivity `[course]`

| Ratio | Definition | What it shows |
|---|---|---|
| Gross margin | (Revenue − cost of goods sold) ÷ sales | Pricing power and production efficiency |
| Operating expense margin | Operating expenses ÷ sales | How well overhead and SG&A are managed; compare over time and with peers |
| PP&E turnover | Sales ÷ average PP&E | Ways to improve it: divest unproductive assets, sale-leaseback, joint ventures, outsourcing |
| Cash conversion cycle | Days inventory outstanding + days sales outstanding − days payables outstanding | Lower generally means the operating cycle turns into cash faster |

## Leverage and coverage `[course]`

| Ratio | Definition |
|---|---|
| Liabilities to equity | Total liabilities ÷ total equity |
| Times interest earned | Earnings before interest and tax (EBIT) ÷ **gross** interest expense |

Common slips to flag: using net interest expense in TIE without saying so; calling debt ÷ equity "liabilities ÷ equity" (they differ when the company has large non-debt liabilities, such as deferred revenue or leases).

## Operating vs. nonoperating return `[course]`

The course also breaks ROE into a return on core operations and a return from financing and investing:

- **ROE = (RNOA + nonoperating return) × NCI ratio**
- **RNOA** (return on net operating assets) = NOPAT ÷ average NOA = NOPM × NOAT
  - **NOPM** (net operating profit margin) = NOPAT ÷ sales
  - **NOAT** (net operating asset turnover) = sales ÷ average NOA
- **NOA** = operating assets − operating liabilities
  - Operating assets include receivables, inventories, prepaid expenses, PP&E and right-of-use assets, intangibles and goodwill, deferred tax assets, equity-method investments.
  - Operating liabilities include payables, accrued expenses, unearned (deferred) revenue, taxes payable, deferred tax liabilities, pension and post-employment obligations.
- **NNO** (net nonoperating obligations) = nonoperating liabilities (debt, lease obligations, interest and dividends payable, derivative liabilities) − nonoperating assets (cash, investments, marketable securities, derivative assets).
- **Leases:** the right-of-use asset is operating; the lease obligation is nonoperating.
- **NOPAT** = net operating profit before tax − tax on operating profit.
  - Net operating profit before tax = sales − operating expenses (COGS, SG&A, R&D, operating impairments, restructuring, other operating items).
  - Tax on operating profit = tax expense + (pretax net nonoperating expense × **statutory** tax rate). The statutory rate is used for the nonoperating tax shield because most tax credits relate to operations.
- **Nonoperating return** = FLEV × spread
  - FLEV = average NNO ÷ average equity (negative for cash-rich companies that are net lenders)
  - Spread = RNOA − NNEP, where NNEP = net nonoperating expense ÷ average NNO

A team that uses the classic three-factor DuPont instead of this operating/nonoperating version isn't wrong, but should name which one it used.

## Using this file when grading

- Recompute at least two ratios per draft using these definitions.
- If a team's number differs only because of a disclosed definition choice, note it as advice, not a deduction.
- If peers and the company are computed on different definitions, that is a comparability finding (checklist item 10).
